Shipped 2026-07-20 — the .dop arc

  • .dop files were being INVENTED, and a day's colour work landed where PhotoLab cannot read it SHIPPED 763e5d5 + cd7c059, user-verified live. Culling a day with the .xmp channel switched off wrote colour to the .dop, and sidecarlib.ensure_dop created one when none existed. One afternoon left 647 SpeciesViewer-authored .dop files (empty Base, schema "19.5") holding 44 Green + 3 Yellow in a form PhotoLab misreads. The skeleton's own comment had called this “the one unverified assumption in this path”; it was later verified and is false — PhotoLab assigns each RAW a random v4 UUID in its own catalog and matches items by it, so a recipe we author is taken as a virtual copy; the UUIDs cannot be computed and the format has no master/virtual flag.

    Fixes. (1) ensure_dop never creates — it returns an existing recipe or none, and the skeleton is deleted, not disabled, since a dormant one invites a retry. (2) Write both channels — colour, rating and crop go to the .xmp always and to the .dop whenever PhotoLab authored one. The old 2×2 switch was a false choice: PhotoLab ignores the .xmp when its metadata sync is off, Lightroom ignores the .dop always, and neither reads the other's file under any setting — so writing both cannot conflict and is the only way they stay in step. Changed in the single gate (utils/sidecar_policy), not at the nine call sites.

    The Sidecar Behaviour dialog lost its four checkboxes — switches that decide nothing are worse than none — and explains the behaviour instead, with the PhotoLab advice. The supported direction is: PhotoLab authors, we edit. Verified live: a day went 36 files (ORF+XMP) → 54 (ORF+DOP+XMP) after Ctrl-A → write sidecars, and our colour then merged into a genuine recipe (Software = "DxO PhotoLab 9.8", full Base, real CafId). Recovery for files already invented: tools/svr_dop_colour_scan.py is read-only, reports what each holds, and tells ours from PhotoLab's (our Software stamp and an empty Base) so real recipes are never swept up with them.

  • The DxO options are one subject, and now one menu SHIPPED 99e5d61 — the PhotoLab path was a lone field at the bottom of Global Settings while Denoise and Sidecar Behaviour sat loose in the Settings menu. Now File ▸ Settings ▸ Interfaces to Lightroom and PhotoLab: PhotoLab Location… (the path moved there; [Global] dxo_path unchanged, and it now says at once whether the path is real — a PhotoLab upgrade renames the folder 9→10, which otherwise only surfaces later as a failure to open a picture), PhotoLab Denoise…, Sidecar Behaviour….
  • After an ingest, offer PhotoLab — and say why SHIPPED 99e5d61 + cd7c059 — a freshly read day has no .dop, and we no longer invent them, so colour and crop set before PhotoLab has seen the day live in the .xmp alone.   • Geotag the master from the virtual-trip map SHIPPED — the map was flatly read-only because an album's files are the master's — which also made it the best surface we have for fixing the master's missing coordinates. Geotagging is now let through, and only geotagging: rating, colour, crop and delete stay refused, and the permission is gated on the machine's existing master write policy, so a read-only master still gets the view-only map. Two rules hold it honest. Fill, never overwrite: you can place a picture that has no coordinate, but you cannot drag a bubble to move pictures whose coordinates the camera recorded. And the map says what it does — where it read “· view only” it now reads “· placing writes to the master”.
    To see where the work is, each day now shows a No GPS count; double-click it to open that day's map. Sri Lanka: 285 of 2060 pictures, with two days consisting of nothing but those — which is precisely why they auto-titled “onbekend”.
  • GPS provenance — telling our coordinates from the camera's SHIPPED — needed before any batch can seed coordinates from a place name, because a seeded picture has GPS and would otherwise be frozen: moving it is exactly what the map forbids. The idea of marking a derived coordinate by making it less precise (whole arcseconds) was tested against the master and 791 of 41,246 GPS'd pictures (1.9%) already look like that — Galapagos 2015 repeating one coordinate across many photos, a South Africa shot at a suspiciously round position. The instinct was right that something is there, but as a permission signal it fails, and it rides on a number that any tool may reformat. So the idea was split into its two true jobs:
    Permission now lives in GPSProcessingMethod, the field whose actual purpose is recording how a fix was obtained. Blank means the camera measured it and we do not touch it; “manual” means a person placed it, so it may be moved and refined.
    Precision is about telling the truth: a place-name look-up is accurate to tens of metres, so its coordinate is rounded to match — and it visibly reads as approximate (47.00" beside a camera's 46.52"), which is the cue that it still wants attention. It never decides what you are allowed to do.
    The map's rule therefore sharpens from “fill, never overwrite” to “never overwrite what we did not put there ourselves”.
  • Get GPS from location SHIPPED — the mirror of Get location, sitting right under it in the same menu. That one turns a coordinate into a place name; this turns the place name a picture already carries into a coordinate. It is the answer for the older trips, whose camera had no GPS but whose files say “Helgoland”.
    All 93 pictures of a day share one written place, so it is one look-up, not 93. Nothing is written until you have seen it: a review table shows the written place, what OpenStreetMap found, the coordinate and how many pictures would get it — untick anything you don't trust. The result is deliberately rough (~30 m) and marked as placed by hand, so the next step is to open the day's map and drag the clusters onto the right cliffs. Seed, then refine.
  • The Helgoland bug hunt SHIPPED — running the feature for real turned up six defects, and they were all the same shape: one truth on disk (the picture's sidecar) and several things reading a stale copy of it. A moved picture snapped back when the map was re-opened (the data was never lost — the map was reading its own out-of-date list). The day table said 93 pictures had no GPS when the true answer was 1. “Map whole trip” opened on an empty world map while the day map showed 92 of them on the island. Underneath, the metadata cache had been poisoned: a geotag writes a coordinate, a method and a place name, but only the place name was recorded — so the cache went on insisting the coordinate wasn't there. And an unexplained three-second delay turned out to be the cache being kept inside Dropbox, where the sync client locked it just as we saved, so the save was lost and that folder re-read every file from scratch, every time. It now lives with all the other caches, outside the synced folder. Placing pictures also got far quicker: dragging 62 of them used to launch the metadata tool 124 times, and now does it twice.
  • A placed JPG-only picture wouldn't stay placed SHIPPED — same shape as the bug hunt above, one more stale copy. On the Tucson 2023 map two birds kept coming back to the “without GPS” tray on every re-open, and the trip's no-GPS count never dropped, even though the coordinate had in fact been written to the file and was sitting there on disk. The catch: those two were JPG-only pictures — a phone or a friend's camera, no RAW and no separate sidecar — so their GPS lives inside the JPG itself. Both things that re-read a placed coordinate only ever looked at the .xmp sidecar and never at the JPG, so a JPG-only picture was invisible to them. They now fall back to the JPG when there's no sidecar. Nothing was ever lost — the placement had always saved; only the reading-back was blind. Confirmed live: the two caracaras drop out of the tray and the day count goes 2 → 0.
  • Find a place by name SHIPPED — we had OpenStreetMap look-up in one direction only (coordinates → place name). 12. Landscapes in the Master NEW ARCHITECTURAL

Raised 2026-07-12 while adding the map's All/Species scope — “Species” drops exactly these pictures, which made their status visible. Today a landscape / context photo is a trip-only, second-class citizen: it never reaches the master.

How it works now

  • A picture is a “blog extra” when its subject carries a marker keyword (landscape / landschap → isBlogExtra). It is deliberately exempted from species resolution (so it doesn't warn as an unknown species), renders in the day blog as a card with no taxon, and is excluded from species groups.
  • It is SKIPPED when freezing to the master — both its picture record and its file records (sv6Freeze), logging “N blog-extra (landscape) pictures excluded”.

Why it is architectural, not a small feature

The master's whole organising principle is the species: the on-disk tree is species folders (TargetPath from the taxonomy chain), and species is the key for the MasterDB index, the counters, “move to species”, the per-species website pages, the best-JPG/rating pick, and the audit/heal tooling. A landscape has no taxon, so there is no answer today to “where does it live and how is it found.”

The real problem (and the reason to do this)

Nothing is lost today — verified: neither delete path touches landscape originals (retire removes the trip's metadata repo, not the picture files; the delete tool only removes a file whose UPN is in the master, and a landscape never is). But landscapes have no path into the master at all, so they are never backed up. After a retire, a landscape survives only as a loose file in the picture root — no master copy, no trip index, and permanently un-cleanable (the cleanup tool reports it “keep — not in master” forever). They accumulate as un-backed-up orphans, one disk failure or one manual cleanup away from being gone.

Direction (user, 2026-07-12): a topics/ branch beside biota/

Add a sibling top-level branch for non-biota subjects — e.g. topics/landscape/<year>/. The organising key stops being species and becomes subject, which resolves either down the biota chain (taxonomy → species) or down a topics chain (topic → leaf/year). TargetPath is already built from a chain, so a topics chain just yields a different root — it gives landscapes a real home, a backup path into the master, and a website home without special-casing them everywhere.

The record is fully reconstructible from disk even for an already-retired trip: subject + GPS/location from the .xmp, rating + colour from the .dop, date/camera/EXIF from the RAW.

⚠ The trap: a scan that reads only the .xmp finds the landscapes but silently loses every rating and colour label — those live in the .dop, not the XMP. Get this right up front.

Open design questions

  • Indexing — MasterDB and the CSV schema assume a species per picture; the generalisation is subject = species OR topic leaf, or species-less records ripple into every species-keyed query, counter, audit and the “move to species” tool.
  • Website — stay blog-only (stored purely for safekeeping) or earn a home (topic pages / a per-year landscape gallery)? Storage and publication are separable.
  • “Best” without a species — the rating/best-JPG pick groups by species; group by topic leaf (year/place), or not at all?
  • Migration — every trip committed so far excluded its landscapes; a backfill would re-ingest them from the picture root.
  • Declaring a topic — today the marker is a hardcoded keyword set; a topics branch needs a small registry (topic → leaf rule) so planes/F16 isn't a code change.

Order of work

The scan cannot go first — there is no TargetPath until a topic has a rule. (1) Topic registry — declarative topic → leaf rule. (2) Scan → dry-run report — walk the picture root with the parallel EXIF reader, listing every topic picture and what was reconstructed from RAW + .xmp + .dop. (3) Reviewed ingest into topics/…, with backup — dry-run → review → explicit apply, never an auto-fix.
