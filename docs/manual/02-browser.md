# 2. Browser

One menu, one idea: **reading a collection**. The same two views over each of the
three scopes that have a collection worth browsing.

These used to be buried one per owning menu — the trip browser under Trips, the
master browser under Master — which hid them behind the pipeline actions they sat
beside. Browsing is not a pipeline step, so it gets its own menu.

| View | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Browser** | The taxonomy tree — Kingdom → Phylum → … → Species. Drill down to a species to see its pictures. |
| **All Species** | A flat, searchable index of every species in the scope. Click one to see its pictures. |

Both are **read-only**. Nothing you do in a browser changes data. To edit, use
the corresponding Species Viewer ([Trips](04-trips.md), [Albums](07-albums.md))
or the master's [Species Viewer](06-master.md).

---

## Trip Browser… / All Species in the Trip…

*Needs: an active trip (or a configured standalone repository).*

The active trip's species, by taxonomy or as a flat list. This shows what the
trip *knows about* — the species it has identified — which is exactly what will
appear on the trip website.

## Album Browser… / All Species in the Album…

*Needs: an active album.*

The same two views over the active album. Remember that an album is a live
date-range window on the master, so what you see here is the master's own
content, filtered to those dates. Nothing is copied.

## Master Browser… / All Species in the Master…

*Needs: a configured master.*

The whole collection. This is the browser you will use most: it is the answer to
"have I photographed this before, and what did I get?"

---

## Choosing between the two views

- Use the **Browser** when the taxonomy itself is the question — *what warblers
  do I have?* — or when you want to see how much of a family you have covered.
- Use **All Species** when you know roughly what you are after and want to type
  it. It has a search box.

Both end at the same place: a species and its pictures.
